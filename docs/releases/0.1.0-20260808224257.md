## What changed

One change, and it is presentation only: **Input Monitoring is now shown as
included with Accessibility rather than as a second permission to grant.**

macOS grants Input Monitoring alongside Accessibility, so an app holding
Accessibility never appears in the Input Monitoring list and has no switch there
to turn on. Both the first-run view and the Settings section previously showed
it as a separate permission reading *Not yet allowed*, which pointed the reader
at a control that does not exist and offered a choice they had already made. Both
now read **Included with Accessibility**, and the explanations say where the
capability comes from. The assurance that Typover does not record keystrokes is
unchanged.

The row was kept rather than removed: Typover distinguishing a real word
completion from pasted text is a disclosure worth making, and deleting the row
to fix its wording would have said less rather than more.

No engine, Bear-integration, overlay, or preference behaviour changed. The diff
against `20260806051920` is confined to permission-row copy and a status helper
in `TypoverApp`.

## Privacy or permission changes

The permissions required are unchanged: Bear integration needs Accessibility,
and Input Monitoring comes with it. What changed is that the app now says so,
instead of implying the writer must grant two things.

Apple Spelling and Apple Intelligence stay on device. OpenAI or Anthropic sees
one completed sentence only after the writer explicitly selects that provider.

## Known limitations

- Bear correction is post-pause and can safely skip when focus, selection,
  timing, text, or geometry is ambiguous.
- Bear squiggles are non-native overlays and hide when their exact anchor is
  not verifiable.
- The fast event-tap lane is research-only; its measured envelope does not
  broaden the public compatibility claim.
- Pointer-only hover timing, Reduced Motion, VoiceOver, and the remaining Bear
  proximity/wrapped-line interaction observations are still release gates.
